Lo que pasó desde el cierre

El lunes Wall Street tuvo su mejor día desde la recuperación de abril de 2025: el S&P 500 cerró con +4.73%, el Nasdaq con +6.51% y Nvidia con +31.5%, tras el regreso de Jensen Huang. Después del cierre, HPE reportó ingresos trimestrales por 14,500 millones de dólares (+50%), arriba de lo esperado, y elevó su guía de 2027 hasta 63,100 millones, pero su guía del primer trimestre quedó un poco abajo de las estimaciones más altas; su director dijo que no financia a nubes de IA ni a clientes de alto riesgo para comprar servidores.

La Guardia Revolucionaria de Irán dijo que tres tanqueros chocaron con minas en Ormuz y se incendiaron, y EE. UU. investiga si Irán estuvo detrás de ciberataques a hasta 50 tanqueros, según Reuters.

Asia, Europa y futuros

Asia cerró al alza: KOSPI +6.32% (5,006.03), Nikkei +3.91% (58,644.63) y Shanghái +0.41%; Hang Seng −0.85%. A las 4:53 CDMX Europa bajaba: Euro Stoxx 50 −0.87%, DAX −0.96%, CAC 40 −0.13% y FTSE 100 −0.30%.

A las 4:58 CDMX los futuros del Nasdaq 100 bajaban 1.75%, los del S&P 500 0.85% y los del Dow 0.42%; el VIX subía a 29.1. En preapertura, Nvidia cedía 5.5%, HPE 11.6% y AMD 4.2%.

El WTI estaba en 79.92 dólares (−0.3%) y el Brent de enero en 89.26 (−0.1%); el oro bajaba 0.7%, la plata 3.2% y el cobre 3.6% a 5.52 dólares por libra.

Qué se espera hoy

A las 9:00 CDMX sale la confianza del consumidor de noviembre del Conference Board en EE. UU., y ADP publica su dato semanal de empleo. Antes de la apertura reportan Best Buy y Dick's Sporting Goods; después del cierre, Dell y HP. En Alemania sale el índice Ifo. El miércoles salen el PCE y el PIB de EE. UU., y el jueves no opera Wall Street.

Para supply chain

Cierra los recargos de combustible de diciembre con el WTI cerca de mínimos de tres meses, pero no relajes los colchones de días en embarques del Golfo: siguen los incidentes con minas. Si compras servidores o laptops, escucha hoy a Dell y HP sobre precios y memoria.

What happened since the close

On Monday Wall Street had its best day since the April 2025 recovery: the S&P 500 closed +4.73%, the Nasdaq +6.51% and Nvidia +31.5%, after Jensen Huang's return. After the close, HPE reported quarterly revenue of $14.5 billion (+50%), above expectations, and raised its 2027 outlook to as much as $63.1 billion, but its first-quarter guidance fell slightly short of the highest estimates; its CEO said it does not finance AI neoclouds or high-risk customers to buy servers.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said three tankers hit mines in Hormuz and caught fire, and the US is investigating whether Iran was behind cyberattacks on as many as 50 tankers, according to Reuters.

Asia, Europe and futures

Asia closed higher: KOSPI +6.32% (5,006.03), Nikkei +3.91% (58,644.63) and Shanghai +0.41%; Hang Seng −0.85%. At 5:53 a.m. ET Europe was lower: Euro Stoxx 50 −0.87%, DAX −0.96%, CAC 40 −0.13% and FTSE 100 −0.30%.

At 5:58 a.m. ET Nasdaq 100 futures were down 1.75%, S&P 500 futures 0.85% and Dow futures 0.42%; the VIX rose to 29.1. In premarket trading, Nvidia fell 5.5%, HPE 11.6% and AMD 4.2%.

WTI stood at $79.92 (−0.3%) and January Brent at $89.26 (−0.1%); gold fell 0.7%, silver 3.2% and copper 3.6% to $5.52 per pound.

What to expect today

At 10:00 a.m. ET the Conference Board's November consumer confidence index comes out in the US, and ADP releases its weekly jobs data. Best Buy and Dick's Sporting Goods report before the open; Dell and HP after the close.

Germany releases its Ifo index. US PCE and GDP come out on Wednesday, and Wall Street is closed on Thursday.

For supply chain

Lock in December fuel surcharges with WTI near three-month lows, but keep buffer days on Gulf shipments: mine incidents continue. If you buy servers or laptops, listen to Dell and HP today on pricing and memory.
